# Founder HQ, Location, and Team-Culture Context

*Last updated: August 10, 2026*

## Status

This document records Alex's current vision and the resulting working recommendation. It is durable context for future chats, not a signed location decision or authorization to spend money.

## Founder vision

Alex wants an in-person company, not a remote-first organization. The envisioned headquarters is a visually striking warehouse-style space that functions simultaneously as:

- a high-performance cold-calling and sales floor for roughly 10–15 people;
- a physical expression of the company brand;
- a recruiting advantage and source of social content;
- a creator/influencer lounge and event venue;
- a community that makes Alex feel engaged, accountable, and proud to lead.

The desired visual language includes brick or an industrial treatment, an open plan, excellent lighting, a company LED sign, modern sit-stand desks, a snack area, and a visible GT3 RS display. Alex also wants a beautiful three-bedroom home nearby, a strong gym, an active social life, and meaningful separation between work and home.

The deeper objective is not merely an attractive office. Alex believes he performs better around an in-person team and wants work, leadership, community, recruiting, content, and lifestyle to reinforce one another.

## Current recommendation

**Choose San Diego as the operating headquarters.**

- Live in North Pacific Beach or Crown Point, with Bird Rock as a more expensive alternative.
- Recruit and socialize through Pacific Beach and the surrounding university ecosystem.
- Put the actual headquarters in Midway, Morena, or the Pacific Highway corridor, close to PB but better suited to flex/warehouse space, parking, roll-up access, and focused work.
- Use Los Angeles as a customer, creator, and partnership market through planned trips, hosted events, and content days rather than carrying LA's full office and payroll cost.
- Keep Miami/Fort Lauderdale as the lower-tax benchmark, not the current cultural recommendation.
- Do not turn Alex's home into a permanent 10–15-person office. Residential use, parking, insurance, privacy, neighbor impact, recruiting optics, and work-life separation all argue for a nearby dedicated headquarters.

This recommendation is based on founder-environment fit, not on San Diego being the cheapest city. Miami generally remains superior on a pure state-tax spreadsheet. San Diego is favored because Alex already knows and likes the lifestyle, values PB's social and recruiting ecosystem, and expects to operate better with a local in-person team.

## Market and talent thesis

Pacific Beach plausibly supplies a useful stream of socially confident, educated, early-career candidates through SDSU, UCSD, USD, Point Loma Nazarene, local transplants, hospitality, fitness, and nightlife. San Diego also has fewer dense clusters of major marketing agencies than Los Angeles, which can make a differentiated local employer more noticeable.

Do not turn that thesis into the unsupported claim that most PB residents are cheap, hungry, loyal, or ready to sell. The practical interpretation is:

- PB is a strong source of **raw, socially skilled trainees**.
- It is not automatically a source of trained outbound sellers.
- San Diego's lower density of competing agencies may reduce immediate poaching, but loyalty must still be earned through compensation, coaching, advancement, competent management, and trust.
- Los Angeles has a larger pool of trained creators, marketers, actors, and agency employees, but also higher competition, compensation expectations, and company-hopping risk.
- Fort Lauderdale may have useful phone-sales pockets, but Alex has a stronger personal connection to San Diego and has not yet established that South Florida offers a superior, repeatable recruiting channel for the specific company.

## Recommended founding sales-floor composition

Do not open with 10–15 untrained hospitality hires. Build capacity for that team, then fill it in cohorts.

Initial six-person model:

1. One legitimate player-coach or floor leader with verified outbound results.
2. Two experienced cold callers from SaaS, recruiting, home services, media, fitness, or another phone-intensive category.
3. Three high-potential trainees recruited from universities, hospitality, gyms, referrals, or local community channels.

Scale toward 12–15 seats only after multiple cohorts demonstrate acceptable ramp time, call quality, retention, contribution margin, and manageable coaching load.

Hire using a consistent scorecard: communication, resilience, competitiveness, coachability, preparation, reliability, professional presentation, ethical judgment, and cold-call work samples.

The company may naturally attract a youthful and energetic workforce, but **"young and attractive" must never become a hiring criterion or job-ad message**. Besides the legal risk, that positioning can make the company resemble an MLM and attract clout-seekers instead of durable performers. California specifically warns against age ranges and phrases such as "young and energetic" in recruiting. Use terms such as high-energy, competitive, articulate, camera-comfortable, and coachable only where they describe actual job requirements.

## Culture design

Build a sports-team operating culture, not a fraternity or permanent party:

- daily huddle and clear goals;
- protected call blocks;
- live coaching and call review;
- a visible, fair performance scoreboard;
- weekly one-on-ones and documented promotion levels;
- meaningful recognition for improvement, quality, teamwork, and revenue;
- voluntary monthly social events that rotate among dinners, fitness, beach activities, Padres games, and occasional nightlife.

Alcohol cannot be the price of belonging. Events should be voluntary, offer nonalcoholic options, include safe transportation, and operate under normal conduct and anti-harassment expectations.

Employee participation in social content should be voluntary and covered by written releases. Keep client information, live calls, screens, and nonparticipating employees out of recordings.

## Content and recruiting flywheel

The headquarters can produce repeatable content, but virality is upside and must not be part of the financial underwriting.

The public story should emphasize:

- real coaching and skill development;
- promotions and credible earnings paths;
- customer outcomes and company competence;
- founder accessibility and participation on the floor;
- creator collaborations and useful events;
- team camaraderie and the San Diego lifestyle.

Avoid making the GT3 RS, attractive employees, uncapped commissions, and nightclub tables the dominant story. Those can be texture, but together they can create scam or MLM optics if not anchored by evidence of a serious company.

## Physical headquarters brief

Target approximately 4,500–5,000 square feet for the mature version:

| Zone | Approximate area | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Acoustically isolated call floor | 1,500–1,800 sq ft | 12–15 sit-stand desks plus coach |
| Creator lounge/content set | 900–1,200 sq ft | Guests, shoots, snack service, events |
| Enclosed vehicle studio | 700–900 sq ft | GT3 RS display with direct exterior roll-up |
| Coaching/private rooms | 250–350 sq ft | Call review, interviews, private calls |
| Kitchen/snack area | 250–350 sq ft | Daily team use and catering |
| Remaining area | Balance | Restrooms, IT, storage, circulation |

Acoustic treatment is essential. Fifteen callers in a hard warehouse shell will create intolerable reverberation without absorptive ceilings or clouds, wall treatment, carpet tile, solid-core doors, and quiet rooms. Also require strong HVAC, dual wired internet where possible, cellular failover, sufficient electrical capacity, 24/7 access, visitor control, and a realistic parking solution.

The preferred design is a glass-separated vehicle bay that is visually connected to the office but operationally separate. A static indoor display can trigger fuel, battery, fire-separation, and movement restrictions. A car driven in and out routinely may require a lawful garage/vehicle-storage use, ventilation, and additional separation. Written landlord, zoning, building/fire, auto-insurer, and commercial-insurer approval is required before treating the car as part of the plan.

## Phased execution

### Phase 1: minimum viable headquarters

- Roughly 2,000–2,500 square feet or a short, expandable flex arrangement.
- Six workstations, one strong branded wall, proper acoustics, a small creator lounge, and a legal vehicle solution only if inexpensive and approved.
- Initial six-person sales-floor composition described above.
- Run long enough to establish repeatable recruiting, ramp, performance, retention, and coaching data.

### Phase 2: flagship headquarters

- Expand to roughly 4,500–5,000 square feet and 12–15 seats only after the sales system is proven.
- Add the full creator lounge, purpose-built content studio, upgraded lighting, glass vehicle bay, and larger events program.

The office should amplify demonstrated momentum rather than serve as a substitute for it.

## Financial frame

All figures are directional and require fresh quotes.

- A roughly $11,000 advertised rent can become approximately $14,000–$18,000 per month after building expenses, utilities, insurance, cleaning, connectivity, and maintenance.
- Furniture, lighting, acoustics, HVAC, electrical work, permits, accessibility, and construction can create a low-to-mid-six-figure initial project.
- Every $10,000 change in average annual compensation changes payroll by $100,000 for ten employees or $150,000 for fifteen.
- The mature 10–15-person headquarters is likely a $1 million-plus annual operating system after compensation, payroll burden, management, recruiting, software, and occupancy. The office rent is not the dominant cost.
- A current August 2026 PB rental snapshot showed several three-bedroom houses around roughly $5,700–$8,200 per month, with premium properties higher. This supports keeping the home separate while remaining below Alex's stated $20,000-per-month ceiling for the residence.

Do not rely on future recruiting virality or unproven rep production to make a long lease affordable. Size the commitment to existing cash flow/capital and require measurable proof before expansion.

## Payroll, intern, and tax guardrails

- A commission-heavy structure does not automatically eliminate California minimum-wage, overtime, meal/rest, sick-leave, recordkeeping, reimbursement, or written commission-agreement duties.
- As of January 1, 2026, the City of San Diego minimum wage is $17.75 per hour for covered work in city limits. Reverify the rate and applicable wage order before hiring.
- Do not treat ordinary interns performing productive company work as free labor. Assume they are paid unless California employment counsel confirms a narrow, fact-specific exception.
- If Alex lives and works in California, plan on genuine California personal residency and California employer obligations. Do not create a paper Miami domicile or questionable deductions that conflict with the facts. Use a California employment lawyer, CPA, and payroll provider to maximize lawful deductions and entity planning.

## ClickUp precedent

ClickUp is a relevant San Diego precedent, with important chronology:

- It was founded in 2017, moved to San Francisco in November 2017, and moved to San Diego in November 2019.
- Its Series A followed in June 2020 and Series B in December 2020, so the San Diego move preceded institutional funding.
- Founder Zeb Evans said a bootstrapped startup could not compete with San Francisco salaries and competition, while San Diego offered California quality of life and people seeking more exciting, higher-growth work. He described San Diego hiring as phenomenal and said the company scaled on-premises customer support there far more effectively.
- ClickUp's 36,500-square-foot East Village headquarters—with open collaboration, standing desks, content space, food, fitness access, and an accessible founder—came after major traction and funding.

The correct lesson is not that an aesthetic office creates success. San Diego improved founder/lifestyle fit and hiring conditions; the large office later amplified a culture and business engine that already existed.
