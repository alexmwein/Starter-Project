# Product Marketing Context

*Last updated: July 24, 2026*

## Product Overview

**Working name:** Third Standard

**One-liner:** A fictional peptide-company website built around evidence-first product dossiers and legible lot documentation.

**Current reality:** Third Standard is a concept website. It has no company operations, inventory, material, released lots, customers, testing, fulfillment, checkout, or commerce. A permanent notice at the top of every page states that the concept is fictional and that nothing described is stocked or offered for sale.

**What the website demonstrates:** A credible future information architecture for evaluating peptide research materials without inventing proof. It presents eight named peptide dossiers, an empty-state Lot Record, research notes, and a local-only research profile flow.

**Concept category:** Peptide research-material catalog and evidence system

**Product type:** Fictional brand prototype and strategic market concept

**Future business hypothesis:** If legal, quality, insurance, banking, testing, and fulfillment gates are independently cleared, the evidence system could support qualified institutional procurement, verification projects, and documentation software. None of those services exists today.

Every dossier must preserve the same evidence boundary:

- no material exists;
- no specification has been released;
- no characterization has been performed;
- no lot exists;
- nothing is available or offered for sale;
- no price, stock state, checkout, quote, waitlist, or delivery promise appears;
- no dosing, administration, preparation, body-outcome, safety, efficacy, or human-use claim appears.

The dossier names make the concept specific. Empty evidence states keep it honest.

## Primary User Journeys

1. **Browse peptides:** Open the peptide index and compare the eight dossiers through a consistent scientific taxonomy.
2. **Inspect the Lot Record:** Review the proposed record structure and see exactly what a future lot record would need to prove. The current record is a demonstration, not a real certificate or released lot.
3. **Read research notes:** Learn how to distinguish specifications, test methods, characterization, release evidence, and evidence boundaries.
4. **Prepare a local research profile:** Enter institutional context in the browser-only profile flow. It is a prototype interaction. It does not submit an application, create an account, contact a company, or initiate commerce.

## Intended Future Audience

**Potential organizations:** U.S. biotechnology startups, contract research organizations, university-affiliated laboratories, analytical labs, and preclinical research teams with a legitimate institutional use case.

**Potential decision-makers:** Principal investigators, lab managers, procurement leads, research directors, quality leads, and founders of early-stage biotechnology companies.

**Potential job to be done:** Evaluate a peptide research material and preserve the evidence needed for internal review without chasing disconnected PDFs, unclear lots, or anonymous support.

| Persona | Cares about | Challenge | Concept value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lab manager | Continuity, lead time, records | Documents and lots are fragmented | One proposed record from review through reorder |
| Research director | Fit-for-purpose evidence | Marketing language obscures specifications | Clear separation between specifications and proof |
| Procurement lead | Entity, terms, support, invoices | Small vendors feel operationally unreliable | A model for qualified purchasing and legible records |
| Quality lead | Chain of custody and exceptions | A purity PDF is mistaken for a quality system | Explicit evidence boundaries and test-status labels |

## Problems and Pain Points

**Core problem:** The market makes peptide research materials easy to list and hard to evaluate.

**Why alternatives fall short:**

- Commodity storefronts optimize fast checkout before purchaser qualification.
- COAs are frequently detached from live inventory or unclear about test scope.
- Large scientific distributors can be slow and impersonal for emerging research programs.
- Consumer-coded brands create reputational and compliance risk for legitimate laboratories.

**Potential cost:** Procurement delays, repeated diligence, failed experiments, support friction, and records that cannot survive internal review.

**Emotional tension:** “Can I defend why we selected this material, this lot, and this supplier?”

## Competitive Landscape

**Observed peptide storefronts:** American Peptides, Modern Peptides, Skye Peptides, Core Peptides, Loti Labs, and similar research-supply sites. They are often strong in one of catalog depth, lot files, content, or conversion, but inconsistent across qualification, evidence boundaries, and professional procurement.

**Scientific references:** Large scientific distributors and custom-synthesis houses offer mature product taxonomies and technical documentation, though they may be slower or less approachable for small teams.

**Indirect alternatives:** Direct overseas sourcing and community group buys reduce apparent cost while increasing diligence and chain-of-custody burden.

## Positioning

**Current positioning:** The documentation architecture is the product demonstration.

**Future positioning hypothesis:** A smaller, more legible peptide catalog could earn trust by placing evidence status beside every decision, not behind a generic footer link.

**Key design principles:**

- Evidence status before promotional claims.
- Lot-specific documentation architecture, without fabricated lots or reports.
- Labels that distinguish identity, specification, characterization, release, and availability.
- A Lot Record designed for audit continuity.
- A scientific taxonomy that makes dossiers predictable and comparable.
- No human-use content, creator transformation claims, dosing content, or preparation kits.

## Anti-Persona

Individual consumers, lifestyle buyers, coaches, clinics seeking non-prescription products, creators promoting body outcomes, resellers, and anyone requesting dosing, preparation, administration, or personal-use advice.

## Customer Language

**Questions the concept is designed to answer:**

- “Which lot would I actually receive?”
- “What exactly was tested?”
- “What does this document prove?”
- “Could procurement defend this vendor decision?”

**Desired future description:**

- “The research supplier that makes the evidence obvious.”
- “A clean procurement record, not another mystery vial.”

**Words to use:** peptide dossier, lot record, specification, evidence, research material, chain of custody, fit for purpose, documentation, exception.

**Words to avoid:** protocol, dose, results, transformation, therapy, patient, wellness, biohack, pharmacy-grade, medical-grade, guaranteed safe.

| Term | Meaning in the concept |
|---|---|
| Peptide dossier | A named, structured page that separates identity, proposed specification, characterization, lot, and availability states |
| Lot Record | A demonstration of the consolidated specification, testing, release, and availability record a future lot would require |
| Evidence boundary | A plain-language statement of what a document or result proves and does not prove |
| Local research profile | A browser-only prototype that prepares institutional context without submitting it anywhere |

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** Exact, restrained, editorial, quietly confident.

**Style:** Short sentences, concrete nouns, visible caveats, no pseudo-scientific hype.

**Personality:** Precise, composed, selective, transparent, human.

## Proof Rules

**Metrics:** None may be claimed until independently verified.

**Customers:** None may be claimed until contracted and approved for reference.

**Testimonials:** None may be fabricated.

**Laboratory evidence:** No test report, COA, specification, lot, or release status may be represented as real while the concept remains fictional.

**Commercial evidence:** No price, stock, fulfillment, customer-service, or delivery claim may be represented as operational.

| Future value theme | Proof required before any claim |
|---|---|
| Lot continuity | Receiving and shipment records tied to released lots |
| Documentation speed | Timestamped support and document-delivery data |
| Quality transparency | Real third-party reports and explicit test-scope labels |
| Fulfillment reliability | Carrier acceptance and delivery data |

## Goals

**Current goal:** Demonstrate a peptide-specific, evidence-first website that feels operationally credible while remaining unmistakably fictional and non-commercial.

**Primary actions:** Browse the peptide index, inspect the Lot Record, read notes, and prepare a local research profile.

**Current metrics:** Concept only. No commercial, inventory, customer, testing, or performance claims.
