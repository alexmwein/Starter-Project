# Product Marketing Context

*Last updated: July 23, 2026*

## Product Overview

**Working name:** Third Standard
**One-liner:** A qualified-access research supply company for teams that need peptide reference materials with documentation they can defend.
**What it does:** Third Standard combines curated research materials, lot-specific evidence, procurement records, and responsive support in one controlled buying experience. It does not sell or promote products for human, clinical, veterinary, cosmetic, or household use.
**Product category:** B2B research materials and analytical reference standards
**Product type:** Qualified-access commerce, procurement service, and documentation portal
**Business model:** Direct B2B sales after account review; custom-synthesis and verification projects; future laboratory-services and software subscriptions

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** U.S. biotechnology startups, contract research organizations, university-affiliated laboratories, analytical labs, and preclinical research teams with a legitimate institutional use case.
**Decision-makers:** Principal investigators, lab managers, procurement leads, research directors, quality leads, and founders of early-stage biotech companies.
**Primary use case:** Source documented peptide research materials without chasing disconnected PDFs, unclear lots, or anonymous support channels.
**Jobs to be done:**

- Evaluate a material before procurement.
- Preserve lot and vendor evidence for internal review.
- Reorder or request a custom specification with less administrative friction.

**Use cases:**

- Analytical method development and reference work.
- In-vitro and preclinical research.
- Custom peptide synthesis for qualified research programs.
- Independent identity, purity, mass, sterility, endotoxin, and related testing coordination where applicable.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** The market makes research materials easy to list and hard to evaluate.
**Why alternatives fall short:**

- Commodity storefronts optimize fast checkout before purchaser qualification.
- COAs are frequently detached from live inventory or unclear about test scope.
- Large catalog distributors can be slow and impersonal for emerging research programs.
- Consumer-coded brands create reputational and compliance risk for legitimate laboratories.

**What it costs them:** Procurement delays, repeated diligence, failed experiments, support friction, and records that cannot survive internal review.
**Emotional tension:** “Can I defend why we bought this material, from this lot, from this supplier?”

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** Modern Peptides, Skye Peptides, Core Peptides, Loti Labs, and similar research-supply stores — often strong in one of catalog depth, lot files, or shipping, but inconsistent across qualification, evidence boundaries, and professional procurement.
**Secondary:** Large scientific distributors and custom synthesis houses — credible and mature, but often slower or less approachable for small teams.
**Indirect:** Direct overseas sourcing and community group-buys — lower apparent cost with much higher diligence and chain-of-custody burden.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**

- Qualification before commerce.
- Lot-specific documentation adjacent to every available material.
- Evidence labels that distinguish identity, purity, mass, sterility, endotoxin, stability, and provenance.
- An immutable-style “Lot Record” designed for audit and reorder continuity.
- Custom procurement support for small research teams.
- No human-use content, creator transformation claims, dosing content, or preparation kits.

**How we do it differently:** The documentation architecture is the product, not a footer link.
**Why that is better:** Buyers can decide faster, retain evidence, and explain the purchase later.
**Why customers choose us:** A smaller, more legible catalog with serious records and reachable support.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| “You are more expensive than an open storefront.” | The price includes qualification, documentation, support, and a defensible procurement trail. |
| “A COA already tells me everything.” | A COA answers only the tests it contains. Third Standard labels what was and was not tested. |
| “Why can’t I check out immediately?” | Controlled access protects legitimate research customers and keeps the catalog aligned with its stated purpose. |

**Anti-persona:** Individual consumers, lifestyle buyers, coaches, clinics seeking non-prescription products, creators promoting body outcomes, resellers, and anyone requesting dosing, preparation, administration, or personal-use advice.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** Unmapped COAs, unclear stock, slow support, noisy catalogs, and reputational risk.
**Pull:** Controlled access, clear evidence, concise selection, lot continuity, and professional help.
**Habit:** Existing vendor familiarity and lower sticker prices.
**Anxiety:** Qualification friction, switching suppliers mid-study, and uncertainty about a newer company.

## Customer Language

**How they describe the problem:**

- “Which lot am I actually receiving?”
- “What exactly was tested?”
- “Can procurement approve this vendor?”
- “Will the same specification be available next month?”

**How they should describe us:**

- “The research supplier that makes the evidence obvious.”
- “A clean procurement record, not another mystery vial.”

**Words to use:** lot record, specification, evidence, qualified access, research material, chain of custody, fit for purpose, documentation, exception.
**Words to avoid:** protocol, dose, results, transformation, therapy, patient, wellness, biohack, pharmacy-grade, medical-grade, guaranteed safe.
**Glossary:**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lot Record | The consolidated specification, testing, release, and availability record for one lot |
| Evidence boundary | A plain-language statement of what a document proves and does not prove |
| Qualified access | Account review based on entity, role, research context, and purchasing purpose |
