# Datum Peptides — End-to-End Marketing Plan

*Current scope: fictional storefront and concept validation. Every revenue tactic is conditional on all launch gates.*

## Executive strategy

Datum should market a better way to find and evaluate peptide concepts, not a medical outcome and not an evidence claim it cannot prove.

The acquisition and conversion system is:

`high-intent discovery → fast search/category match → compact PDP decision block → explicit testing state → demo add → local cart → concept-learning loop`

During the concept phase, the final conversion is a measured interaction, not a transaction. There is no checkout, inventory, order, shipping, or sales follow-up.

## Positioning

### Category

Search-first peptide ecommerce concept.

### Promise

Find the compound. See the state. Decide without the noise.

### Core contrast

Most peptide stores maximize claims and merchandising density; many scientific catalogs maximize documentation density. Datum combines the product-finding speed of modern ecommerce with the plain evidence boundaries of a careful research interface.

### Reasons to believe today

- Visible, predictive product search.
- Compact category and product navigation.
- Consistent PDP decision blocks.
- Testing status at the decision point.
- Fast static experience.
- Working local demo cart.
- No fabricated reviews, labs, purity, stock, shipping, or urgency.

These are observable product attributes, not operational proof.

## Audiences

### Primary concept-testing audience

Ecommerce shoppers and research-material buyers who can assess search, collection, PDP, testing, and cart usability without assuming the site is an operating vendor.

### Secondary audience

Ecommerce operators, CRO specialists, designers, and research-procurement professionals evaluating the concept’s information architecture.

### Conditional future ICP

Only after written legal and operating approval: qualified organizations with a legitimate research-material need, documented purchasing authority, and the ability to satisfy the approved access model.

### Excluded audience and intent

- people seeking personal use;
- medical, treatment, dosing, administration, or preparation guidance;
- clinics or creators seeking unapproved consumer outcomes;
- resellers or affiliates centered on human outcomes;
- anyone attempting to bypass an approved access policy.

## Jobs to be done

1. “Take me to the compound I already know.”
2. “Help me scan the catalog without decoding the vendor’s taxonomy.”
3. “Tell me what evidence exists before I commit attention.”
4. “Let me compare product concepts and a set without losing context.”
5. “Explain a technical distinction in plain language.”

## Message architecture

### Master message

Peptide product discovery should be fast. Testing states should be honest.

### Supporting messages

- Search is always one action away.
- Every card answers the same decision questions.
- Every PDP puts product, concept price, testing state, and action together.
- “No result reported” is more useful than a generic trust badge.
- The demo cart is complete enough to evaluate and deliberately stops before checkout.

### Proof rule

Every sentence must be one of:

1. directly observable in the interface;
2. cited to an authoritative source;
3. labeled as an assumption or hypothesis;
4. removed.

## Website conversion system

### Global header

- Thin fiction banner above everything.
- Sticky store header.
- Datum wordmark.
- Exposed search input with predictive results.
- Primary links: Shop, Bundles, Testing & COAs, Learn.
- Cart count based only on browser-local demo state.
- Category subnavigation: Shop All, GLP-1 & Metabolic, Research Peptides, Growth Hormone Research, Blends.

### Homepage sequence

1. **Hero:** clear category statement, Shop concepts action, visible product imagery, product/testing preview.
2. **Trust strip:** interface truths only—fast search, honest testing states, local demo cart.
3. **Category tiles:** immediate self-selection.
4. **Product row:** first products visible within the first desktop viewport and near the first mobile scroll.
5. **Testing feature:** a document-like “No result reported” example.
6. **Concept sets:** transparent included items and arithmetic.
7. **Learn:** three concise guides tied to catalog decisions.
8. **Newsletter demo:** local success state; nothing transmitted.
9. **Compact footer:** routes, fiction state, no operational claims.

### Collection page

- Visible result count and category title.
- Search within results.
- Multi-select category filters where useful.
- Clear applied-filter state.
- Sort by featured, name, and concept price.
- Four-up desktop grid.
- Two-up mobile grid with readable product names and full-width Add controls.
- No ratings, inventory, sale badges, popularity claims, or “best seller” labels.
- Zero-results state that preserves the search term and offers clear reset/category paths.

### Product card

Each card contains:

1. product image;
2. category;
3. name and concept format;
4. neutral one-line descriptor;
5. testing micro-row: “Testing: no result reported”;
6. concept price;
7. full-width Add control.

### PDP decision block

The first decision region must contain:

- breadcrumb;
- name and product code;
- category and concept format;
- neutral descriptor;
- concept price;
- testing state;
- Add control;
- explicit concept-price and no-sale context accessible without hunting.

Below it:

- testing and report-state explanation;
- concept specifications;
- expandable product-record fields;
- related concepts;
- no claims about use, outcomes, safety, or efficacy.

### Testing & COAs

- Search by product name or code.
- Return a product identity match even when evidence is absent.
- Default state: “No result reported.”
- Explain that no laboratory, method, sample, lot, report date, or result exists.
- Show the fields a future report would require without drawing a fake chromatogram or certificate.
- Never imply that a generic document supports a product.

### Demo cart

- Add, remove, quantity, subtotal, and clear all work.
- Cart persists locally.
- Cart state is announced accessibly.
- No checkout control.
- End state: “This is where the demo ends. Nothing is stocked, sold, or shippable.”

## CRO research translated into decisions

| Evidence | Datum decision | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|
| Baymard reports 58% of desktop and 78% of mobile stores have poor-to-mediocre product-list UX | Treat collection layout, filters, applied state, sorting, and thumbnails as a core product | B — independent large-scale usability benchmark |
| Oaktree Memorials reports +3.34% CVR and +9.93% RPV after exposing mobile search | Keep search visibly exposed rather than icon-only | C — agency/client case study; directional |
| Oaktree reports +28% mobile CVR for collection discovery variants | Use visual category tiles and category-specific collection entry | C — case study; test locally |
| Oaktree reports +13% RPV from expanded PDP accordion navigation | Use concise, scannable PDP accordions | C — case study; test locally |
| Gym King reports incomplete collection ratings reduced RPV by 10% | Do not display ratings until coverage and verification are real | C — vendor case study |
| Gym King reports two complete mobile products and a simplified cart among winning improvements | Design for two complete cards per mobile viewport and remove cart clutter | C — vendor case study |
| Nuvemshop reports LCP health improving from 57% to 96%, with +8.9% mobile organic conversion and +8.4% cart engagement | Do not lazy-load or animate the above-fold LCP image; use explicit priority | B/C — first-party platform case study with large cohort |
| Inherited CRO research reports predictive search materially outperformed basic search | Implement predictive results and measure search-to-PDP rate | C/D — secondary case evidence; raw data unavailable |

No external lift is a forecast for Datum.

## Experiment backlog

### Priority 0 — release guardrails

- Fiction banner comprehension.
- Search works for every product name, code, category, and common abbreviation.
- No-result recovery.
- Mobile product-card completeness.
- PDP decision-block visibility.
- Cart truth-state comprehension.
- Performance and accessibility.

### Priority 1 — discovery

| Test | Hypothesis | Primary metric | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Search placeholder | Product examples improve initiation | Search-start rate | Zero-result rate |
| Predictive result density | Four results outperform a long panel | Search-result selection | Search abandonment |
| Category tiles | Visual categories beat text links | Category-to-PDP rate | Homepage LCP |
| Card density | Four-up desktop increases product reach | Product views/session | Card readability |
| Mobile density | Two-up outperforms one-up | Product views/session | Mis-taps / task success |

### Priority 2 — decision

| Test | Hypothesis | Primary metric | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Testing state placement | State beside price reduces uncertainty | Testing open / demo add | Misunderstanding rate |
| PDP accordion labels | Question-led labels improve engagement | Accordion open rate | Time to Add |
| Related concepts | Category-adjacent products improve exploration | Related PDP click | Back exits |
| Concept-set anatomy | Visible included items improve confidence | Set demo-add rate | Item comprehension |

### Priority 3 — cart

| Test | Hypothesis | Primary metric | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cart opens after Add | Immediate feedback improves comprehension | Cart engagement | Continued browsing |
| Drawer return path | Closing the cart back to the originating control preserves flow | Second product view | Add confusion |
| Truth copy position | End-state copy near subtotal is most legible | Fiction comprehension | Demo-add rate |

|---|---|

| `search_submitted` | normalized_query, result_count |

| `sort_changed` | sort_value |
| `product_viewed` | product_code, category, source |
| `testing_opened` | product_code, source |
| `demo_add` | product_code, source, concept_price |
| `cart_opened` | item_count, source |
| `cart_changed` | product_code, action, quantity |
| `cart_cleared` | prior_item_count |
| `article_viewed` | article_slug, pillar |
| `article_product_clicked` | article_slug, product_code |

### Privacy boundaries

- Do not capture personal health, condition, intended human use, dosing, or research protocol.
- Do not send cart contents to advertising platforms.
- Do not treat a concept product view as medical or health-interest data.
- Use aggregate reporting and the minimum retention period.
- Newsletter demo remains local until a reviewed privacy and consent system exists.

### Funnel dashboards

1. Homepage → search/category → PDP → demo add → cart open.
2. Collection → filter/sort → PDP → demo add.
3. Testing page → product match → PDP.
4. Article → category/PDP.
5. Mobile versus desktop.
6. New versus returning, without identity stitching.

## Search strategy

### Current concept

- Index brand, UX, testing-literacy, and neutral product-record content.
- Add clear title, description, canonical, breadcrumb, and article metadata.
- Keep Product and Offer schema absent.
- Create one strong page per intent; avoid mass-generated molecule pages.
- Do not target “buy,” dosing, reconstitution, body outcome, weight loss, or treatment queries.

### Conditional future

Keyword and campaign activation require legal review of exact query, landing page, ad copy, catalog, geography, and audience. A “research use only” negative keyword or disclaimer is not a substitute for compliant intended use.

## Lifecycle plan

There is no commercial lifecycle today.

1. Finds a product or guide.
2. Tests search or category navigation.
3. Reviews a PDP and testing state.
4. Uses the demo cart.

1. Evidence/education discovery.
2. Approved qualification flow.

## Governance

### Required reviewers

- Fable 5: all outward-facing visual decisions.
- Product owner: behavior and commercial logic.
- Claims owner: factual support and evidence state.
- Accessibility reviewer: keyboard, semantics, contrast, and assistive technology.
- Legal counsel: any future commercial, intended-use, product, advertising, or qualification flow.

### Publication checklist

- Is the page clearly within the fictional concept?
- Is every control functional?
- Is each claim observable, cited, labeled, or removed?
- Does any phrase imply stock, sale, shipping, test, laboratory, purity, safety, or outcome?
- Are search intent and next action clear?
- Is desktop and narrow-width evidence reviewed?
- Are Product, Offer, availability, and AggregateRating schema absent?
- Are performance budgets intact?
