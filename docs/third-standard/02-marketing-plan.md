# Third Standard — Full Marketing Plan

## Positioning

### Category

Qualified-access research materials and analytical reference standards.

### Promise

Research materials you can defend.

### Core contrast

Most suppliers show a catalog and attach a PDF. Third Standard begins with the evidence trail and lets qualified teams procure from there.

### Primary conversion

Research-access application after all launch gates pass.

### Secondary conversions

- Download the buyer’s lot-review checklist.
- Subscribe to Evidence Notes.
- Request a custom-synthesis review.
- Open a sample Lot Record schema.

## Ideal customer profile

### Tier 1

Early-stage biotech and CRO teams with 5–100 employees, limited procurement overhead, and real in-vitro or preclinical programs.

### Tier 2

University-affiliated or independent analytical laboratories that can document institutional use.

### Tier 3

Established life-science organizations with a need for responsive custom procurement. These teams have higher requirements and longer sales cycles.

### Excluded demand

Consumers, coaches, bodybuilding communities, wellness clinics seeking non-prescription material, resellers, and creators whose content centers on personal outcomes.

## Offer ladder

1. **Free:** Lot Review Checklist and Evidence Notes.
2. **Qualified access:** Curated analytical reference materials.
3. **Project:** Custom synthesis and verification coordination.
4. **Enterprise:** Procurement continuity, reserved lots, and documentation support.
5. **Software:** Lot Record portal and supplier evidence workflow.

## Website conversion plan

### Homepage

Make the argument in this order:

1. The material is only one part of the purchase.
2. Every lot should arrive with a legible evidence boundary.
3. Third Standard is built for qualified research teams.
4. Show the Lot Record schema.
5. Explain access.
6. Invite the visitor to inspect the standard before applying.

### Research access page

- State who qualifies and who does not.
- Ask for entity, role, research setting, intended analytical context, and purchasing authority.
- Explain manual review and record retention.
- Never imply that checking a box converts consumer intent into research intent.

### Quality page

- Explain each test independently.
- Show what identity, purity, mass, sterility, and endotoxin do and do not establish.
- Distinguish supplier documents, independent reports, and internal release decisions.
- Publish deviations and withdrawals when real.

### Catalog

- Show material class, specification, availability state, and evidence state.
- Do not show fake inventory, fabricated lots, or invented COAs.
- Keep concept catalog entries labeled as examples until launch.

## Launch sequence

### Private validation

- 20 interviews.
- 10 founding-lab letters.
- Five full procurement walkthroughs.

### Evidence preview

- Publish doctrine, checklist, and sample schema.
- Run two expert webinars.
- Invite 50 qualified subscribers, not 5,000 consumers.

### Founding lab pilot

- Admit 10 accounts.
- White-glove all quotes and purchase orders.
- Publish anonymized operational metrics only with permission.

### Controlled public launch

- Open research-access applications.
- Publish the benchmark.
- Announce through partners and professional networks.

## Measurement

### North star

Qualified gross contribution from retained research accounts.

### Leading indicators

- qualified visits;
- checklist completion;
- access application completion;
- acceptance rate;
- quote requests;
- quote-to-order conversion;
- time to first order.

### Retention

- 30/60/90-day reorder;
- document portal return rate;
- support satisfaction;
- reserved-lot renewal;
- custom-project expansion.

### Guardrails

- consumer-intent application rate;
- prohibited-content referrals;
- unsupported claim incidents;
- documents delivered against wrong lot;
- unresolved deviations;
- processor or banking questions caused by unclear disclosure.
