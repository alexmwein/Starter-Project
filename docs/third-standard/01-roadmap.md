# Third Standard — 365-Day Roadmap

## Phase 0 — Permission to exist (days 0–30)

### Legal and operating gates

- Retain specialized FDA/FDCA, customs/import, and product-liability counsel.
- Form the exact legal entity and disclose beneficial ownership.
- Define the initial material list and receive written classification guidance for each.
- Obtain product-liability, general-liability, cyber, cargo, and recall coverage quotes.
- Obtain truthful written approval from banking, payments, and fulfillment counterparties.
- Select a U.S. testing partner and define release panels by material class.
- Write recall, complaint, adverse-information, deviation, and document-retention procedures.

### Market validation

- Interview 20 lab managers, research directors, and procurement leads.
- Secure 10 non-binding founding-lab letters of interest.
- Test willingness to pay for documentation, not only the material.
- Validate which evidence fields reduce procurement friction.

### Exit gate

Proceed only if counsel and counterparties approve a narrow operating model and at least 10 qualified teams validate the problem.

## Phase 1 — Evidence-first pilot (days 31–75)

- Contract two independently diligenced suppliers for redundancy.
- Receive pilot lots into controlled custody.
- Run identity, purity, mass, and material-specific release panels.
- Build one Lot Record per released lot.
- Complete fulfillment dry runs and recall simulation.
- Launch the public education site and private account application.
- Admit no more than 10 founding laboratory accounts.
- Fulfill purchase orders manually with dual review.

### Exit gate

- 100% orders mapped to released lots.
- 100% documents delivered with shipment record.
- Zero unsupported claims.
- Reconciliation closes within five business days.
- No unresolved severity-one quality or compliance event.

## Phase 2 — Controlled commercial launch (days 76–120)

- Expand to 25 approved accounts.
- Publish the first Evidence Notes articles.
- Add reorder and document-notification workflows.
- Introduce custom-synthesis project intake.
- Publish a transparent test-scope matrix.
- Measure quote-to-order, on-time delivery, support response, reorder, and document access.

### Exit gate

- At least 40% of qualified quotes convert.
- At least 25% of eligible accounts reorder inside 90 days.
- Gross contribution covers verification, support, and exception reserves.
- No account entered through consumer or lifestyle intent.

## Phase 3 — Build the operating system (months 5–8)

- Turn Lot Records into a customer portal.
- Add role-based document access and immutable event history.
- Add supplier scorecards and deviation tracking.
- Add procurement integrations and purchase-order support.
- Hire a quality/operations lead before catalog expansion.
- Offer verification coordination as a separate service.

## Phase 4 — Expand the durable layer (months 9–12)

- Reach 75 qualified accounts.
- Add only materials that pass the same legal and quality gates.
- License Lot Records to one adjacent research supplier or CRO.
- Launch a research procurement benchmark report.
- Evaluate diagnostic, assay, and licensed-care partnerships as separate businesses.
- Decide whether to own synthesis capacity, remain asset-light, or deepen software.

## Weekly CEO scorecard

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Approved accounts | Qualified demand, not raw traffic |
| Application acceptance rate | Audience quality and targeting |
| Quote-to-order rate | Commercial fit |
| Contribution per order | Funds evidence and support |
| On-time-in-full rate | Operating reliability |
| Lot-document match rate | Core promise |
| Support first-response time | Trust and retention |
| 30/60/90-day reorder | Durable demand |
| Open deviations by severity | Quality risk |
| Unsupported-claim incidents | Compliance control |

## Kill criteria

Pause or close the material-supply business if any of the following persists:

- counterparties require material misclassification;
- qualified demand cannot support verification costs;
- supplier chain of custody cannot be established;
- banking or insurance approval depends on omitted facts;
- customer demand is predominantly personal use;
- a severe quality event cannot be traced and contained;
- counsel determines the model cannot operate as designed.
