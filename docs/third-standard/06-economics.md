# Third Standard — Illustrative Economics

These are decision models, not forecasts.

## Pilot assumptions

| Variable | Conservative | Base | Strong |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Approved accounts by month 6 | 15 | 25 | 40 |
| Orders per active account/month | 0.8 | 1.2 | 1.6 |
| Average order value | $650 | $900 | $1,250 |
| Material gross margin | 35% | 45% | 55% |
| Verification + fulfillment/order | $140 | $125 | $115 |
| Support + payment/order | $65 | $55 | $50 |

At the base case:

- 25 accounts × 1.2 orders × $900 = $27,000 monthly revenue.
- 45% material gross margin = $12,150.
- 30 orders × $180 verification, fulfillment, support, and payment cost = $5,400.
- Illustrative monthly contribution = $6,750 before payroll, insurance, legal, software, tax, and exceptions.

The supply business should not be scaled on the assumption that commodity markup alone funds a serious quality system.

## Durable revenue targets

- Custom synthesis project management: fixed project fee plus disclosed procurement margin.
- Verification coordination: per-lot fee.
- Reserved-lot continuity: annual account program.
- Lot Record software: annual B2B subscription after workflow validation.

## Capital discipline

- Begin with customer-backed or reserved inventory where possible.
- Keep no more than 60–90 days of expected qualified demand without a documented reason.
- Do not treat retail list value as inventory value.
- Fund verification, insurance, legal, recall reserves, and working capital before distributions.
- Do not underwrite long-term value from a short regulatory window.
