Evidence Note 001 · 2026

What a certificate proves—and what it does not.

A certificate becomes dangerous when confidence spreads beyond the question its method actually answered.

A certificate of analysis is not one category of evidence. It is a container. Its value depends on the sample, the method, the laboratory, the original data, the acceptance criteria, and the chain connecting the tested sample to the material in front of the buyer.

Read every result as a narrow answer to a narrow question.

Identity is not purity.

A chromatographic purity figure describes the relative profile observed under a particular method. It does not, on its own, establish that the principal peak is the named material. Identity needs its own fit-for-purpose evidence.

Purity is not quantity.

A material can present a high relative purity and still contain less or more total material than the label states. Measured mass or content is a separate question with its own units, uncertainty, sampling, and acceptance criteria.

A tested sample is not automatically the shipped lot.

The report matters only if the sample identity can be traced through receipt, sampling, storage, release, and shipment. A detached PDF can be genuine while remaining irrelevant to the material delivered.

One clean result is not a quality system.

A defensible operation also preserves specification changes, deviations, complaints, retests, withdrawals, supplier changes, and disposition decisions. The uncomfortable records are often the ones that prove the system is real.

The practical rule.

For each claim, ask: what exactly was tested, by whom, using which method, against which boundary, on which sample, and how is that sample connected to the lot under review?

That question set is the foundation of the Third Standard Lot Record.
